Anaemia
Overview:
Anaemia is a condition in which the number of red blood cells or the amount of haemoglobin in the blood is lower than normal. Haemoglobin is responsible for carrying oxygen throughout the body. When oxygen supply is reduced, it can lead to fatigue and weakness.
Symptoms:
Common symptoms of anaemia include tiredness, weakness, dizziness, shortness of breath, pale skin, and reduced physical capacity. Children and pregnant women may experience more severe effects.
Causes:
Anaemia can occur due to:
* Iron, vitamin B12, or folic acid deficiency
* Blood loss
* Chronic diseases or infections
* Genetic conditions affecting red blood cells
Risk Factors:
People at higher risk include children, adolescent girls, pregnant women, older adults, individuals with poor nutrition, and those with chronic illnesses.
Prevention:
Anaemia can often be prevented through:
* Eating iron-rich foods such as leafy vegetables, legumes, meat, and fortified cereals
* Consuming vitamin C-rich foods to improve iron absorption
* Maintaining a balanced and nutritious diet
Complications:
If untreated, anaemia can affect physical growth, cognitive development, work productivity, and overall health.
