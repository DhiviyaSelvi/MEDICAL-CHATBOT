Foodborne diseases

Overview
Foodborne diseases, commonly known as food poisoning, occur after consuming food contaminated with bacteria, viruses, parasites, or harmful chemicals. Contamination can happen at any stage of food production, processing, storage, or preparation. These illnesses mainly affect the digestive system and are a major public health concern worldwide, especially among young children and people in low- and middle-income countries.

Symptoms
Symptoms depend on the type of germ consumed and may appear within a few hours or several days.
Common symptoms include:
* Diarrhea
* Stomach pain or cramps
* Nausea
* Vomiting
* Fever
Most cases are mild, but some can become serious if dehydration occurs.

Causes
Foodborne diseases are caused by eating contaminated food or drinks. Common causes include:
* Bacteria such as Salmonella and E. coli
* Viruses and parasites
* Chemical contaminants such as heavy metals
* Unsafe food handling, storage, or processing
* Environmental contamination of water, soil, or air

Prevention
Foodborne diseases can be prevented by following safe food practices:
* Wash hands thoroughly before handling food
* Cook food completely, especially meat and eggs
* Wash fruits and vegetables properly
* Store food at safe temperatures
* Avoid consuming spoiled or uncovered food
* Drink clean and safe water
* Maintain cleanliness in food preparation areas

Complications
Severe food poisoning can lead to dehydration and other serious health problems. Medical attention is needed if symptoms include:
* Bloody diarrhea
* Diarrhea lasting more than 3 days
* High fever
* Continuous vomiting
* Signs of dehydration such as reduced urination or dizziness
