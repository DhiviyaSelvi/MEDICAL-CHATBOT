INFLUENZA

Overview
Influenza (flu) is an acute respiratory infection caused by influenza viruses and occurs worldwide. It spreads easily through coughs and sneezes. Most people recover within a week without treatment, but severe illness can occur, especially in high-risk groups. Vaccination is the most effective way to prevent influenza.

Symptoms
Symptoms usually appear about 2 days after infection and include:
* Sudden fever
* Dry cough
* Sore throat
* Runny nose
* Headache
* Muscle and joint pain
* Fatigue and severe malaise
In severe cases, influenza can lead to pneumonia, sepsis, or worsen existing chronic diseases.

Causes
Influenza is caused by influenza viruses transmitted through airborne droplets or contaminated surfaces.
There are four types of influenza viruses:
* Type A: Causes seasonal epidemics and pandemics
* Type B: Causes seasonal outbreaks
* Type C: Causes mild illness
* Type D: Affects animals, not humans
People can spread the virus from 1 day before symptoms appear to about 5–7 days after.

Prevention
* Annual influenza vaccination
* Regular handwashing
* Covering mouth and nose while coughing or sneezing
* Staying home when unwell
* Avoiding close contact with infected individuals
Vaccination is especially important for pregnant women, young children, elderly people, individuals with chronic illnesses, and healthcare workers.

Complications
Influenza can cause serious complications such as pneumonia and sepsis, particularly in older adults, young children, and people with weak immune systems. Severe cases may require hospitalization.
