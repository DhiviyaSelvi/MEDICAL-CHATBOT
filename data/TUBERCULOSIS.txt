Tuberculosis (TB) is a contagious bacterial infection caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis. It mainly affects the lungs but can also affect other parts of the body such as the kidneys, spine, and brain. TB spreads through the air when an infected person coughs, sneezes, or talks.

Causes

Tuberculosis is caused by the bacterium Mycobacterium tuberculosis. The disease spreads through airborne droplets when a person with active TB coughs, sneezes, or speaks. People who spend long periods of time near an infected person have a higher risk of infection.

Symptoms

Common symptoms of tuberculosis include:

• Persistent cough lasting more than two weeks
• Chest pain
• Coughing up blood or sputum
• Fever
• Night sweats
• Fatigue and weakness
• Unexplained weight loss
• Loss of appetite

Risk Factors

Certain conditions increase the risk of developing tuberculosis:

• Weakened immune system
• HIV infection
• Malnutrition
• Diabetes
• Living in crowded environments
• Close contact with a person who has active TB

Prevention

Tuberculosis can be prevented through several measures:

• Vaccination with the BCG vaccine
• Early detection and treatment of infected individuals
• Covering the mouth while coughing or sneezing
• Maintaining good ventilation in living spaces
• Avoiding close contact with infected individuals

Treatment

Tuberculosis is treatable with a combination of antibiotics taken for several months. Common medications used include isoniazid, rifampicin, ethambutol, and pyrazinamide. Completing the full course of treatment is essential to prevent drug-resistant TB.
